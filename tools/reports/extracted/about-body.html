Four Generations

Four generations. Still family run.

Reynolds Antique House began in 1939, built to furnish Broadway's most fabled productions, starting with Pygmalion and later My Fair Lady and The King and I. What started as a resource for the stage grew into one of the world's premier decorative arts destinations for collectors, designers, and the trade.

Leadership has passed hand to hand ever since: founder Meyer Newman, to his son Bruce Newman, to his grandson Lewis Baer, to today's fourth-generation CEO, Jake Baer. Every piece Reynolds Antique House handles now, whether bound for sale, auction, or a film set, moves through a single home in Long Island City.

1939 to Today

Eighty-seven years, one family.

From a table of twenty antiques on 47th Street to a single Long Island City hub running three businesses.

1939
Meyer Newman opens Reynolds Antique House
Twenty antiques from his own home, displayed on a table in an empty storefront, built to furnish Broadway productions like Pygmalion.
1940s–60s
Broadway, then television
Reynolds Antique House furnishes The King and I and The Sound of Music, then becomes a main prop source for New York's earliest live TV.
1975
Bruce Newman becomes CEO
Meyer's son inherits the company, keeping it in the family for a second generation.
1977
One roof, for the first time
Reynolds Antique House moves to 425 East 53rd Street, a six-story building, the first time the full inventory is stored under a single roof.
1998
Reynolds Antique House goes online
Reynolds Antique House.com launches, the first antiques dealer to publish prices, images, and measurements on the web. Forbes names it "Best of the Web" in 2000.
2001
Lewis Baer becomes CEO
Meyer's grandson buys the company from his uncle Bruce, becoming Reynolds Antique House's third-generation CEO.
2015
The move to Long Island City
Reynolds Antique House relocates its warehouse operations to Long Island City, the seed of today's single hub.
2016
Jake Baer becomes CEO. Reynolds Antique House Props is born.
Lewis's son becomes Reynolds Antique House's fourth-generation CEO, and Reynolds Antique House Props launches, formally separating the rental business.
2023
The archive grows
Reynolds Antique House acquires Gary Rubinstein Antiques and Modern Design, folding another storied collection into the Reynolds Antique House archive.
2024
Reynolds Antique House Auctions holds its first sale
Reynolds Antique House enters the auction world on November 21, alongside the debut of Reynolds Antique House Contemporary Artists.
Today
One address. Three businesses.
Gallery, auctions, and rentals now run from a single hub at 32-00 Skillman Avenue, Long Island City.
